Every day at midnight Hawaiʻi time, everyone gets the same five places somewhere in the islands.

You're dropped into a view of each spot from the sky, over real terrain. Turn, tilt, look closely. You can't move, so the clues are what's in front of you: the shape of a ridge, a reef, a road, a shoreline. When you think you know where you are, open the map, drop your pin, and lock it in.

The closer you are, the more points. Up to 5,000 a round, 25,000 a day. The right island matters most. Getting close on the right island is where the big points live.

HOW IT WORKS
• Five rounds a day, the same five for everyone
• One try per day, so the leaderboard is fair
• Daily and monthly boards
• Share your score without giving away the answers, with a card that shows your day
• Practice mode with hundreds of extra spots, filter by island or difficulty
• Stats: streaks, best day, and the islands you know best
• Optional daily reminder, scheduled on your phone

MADE FOR THE ISLANDS
Place names are written the way they should be, with the ʻokina and kahakō. Spots near heiau, burial sites, and other wahi pana stay out of the game. Kilo is a small way to spend time with ʻāina, learn place names, and have fun every day.

FREE, OPEN SOURCE, ZERO TRACKING
Kilo is free and the whole thing is open source under the MIT license, on GitHub for anyone to read, run, or improve. There is no tracking of any kind: no analytics, no advertising ID, no location permission. Pick a name and play. No email, no password. Delete your scores and name from inside the app any time.

Imagery is from USGS The National Map, terrain from AWS Open Data, and the map from OpenStreetMap contributors and OpenFreeMap.

Kilo means to watch, to observe, to study the signs. Think you know the islands?
